# Reference study

This study records behaviours and relevance, not assets or recipes. None of the references were copied, scraped or hotlinked. Every concept is an original procedural interpretation of the brief.

## Lagos and Nigerian visual culture

| Reference | Behaviour observed | Relevance to Airix | What was deliberately not copied | Concept informed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lagos street signage and hand-painted instructions | Direct language, sharp contrast, urgency, compression and public/private voice overlap | Suggests interfaces that communicate under pressure | Letterforms, phrases, misspellings and individual artworks | B, C |
| Danfo visual culture | Yellow as a moving identifier; personal lettering over a shared transport type | A strong model for recognisable systems with local variation | Bus silhouettes, slogans and decorative lettering | B |
| Bridges, road networks and junctions | Large civic lines create both connection and friction | A useful spatial metaphor for interoperable digital systems | Literal map traces and identifiable bridge geometry | A |
| Lagoon, ferries and wakes | Movement is routed across two different urban surfaces: road and water | Makes flow and handoff tangible without using dashboard graphics | Ferry photography, route maps and tourism imagery | A, B |
| Market stalls and kiosks | Dense improvisation sits on repeatable structural frames; transactions remain legible | Supports Airix’s Build / Run / Rescue operating logic | Specific stall designs, trader imagery and romanticised “informality” | B, C |
| Lagos nightlife | Light becomes signal, depth and operational evidence rather than decoration | Fits ongoing operations and the reality that systems continue after launch | Club aesthetics, neon gradients and nightlife photography | A |
| [The NATIVE](https://thenativemag.com/) | Strong editorial image hierarchy, direct cultural voice, and flexible story density | Shows how local confidence can precede explanation | Masthead, editorial layouts, images and copy | B, C |
| [ART X Lagos](https://www.artxlagos.com/) | Cultural programming is organised as a bold multidisciplinary system | Useful precedent for range without reducing everything to service cards | Identity system, exhibition imagery and event graphics | C |
| [Wafflesncream](https://www.wafflesncream.com/) | Product, skate culture and Lagos presence are communicated without over-explaining place | Encourages confident material specificity | Commerce UI, photographs, marks and campaign language | B |
| [Orange Culture](https://orangeculture.com.ng/pages/about) | Nigerian fashion identity operates globally without flattening its origin | Supports international ambition with local authorship | Fashion imagery, campaign styling and identity | C |
| Alára | Architecture, material and curation do more work than descriptive copy | Supports premium restraint and physical spatial rhythm | Store architecture, object photography and identity | A, C |

Supporting public context included the [MIT Atlas of Popular Transport’s Lagos mapping](https://atlasofpopulartransport.mit.edu/lagos), [Osaze Amadasun’s Danfo lettering study](https://www.osazeamadasun.com/portfolio/danfo-art), and [Mr. Danfo’s Signs in the Cityscape notes](https://mrdanfo.com/portfolios/). These reinforced that the city’s visual systems are structured, communicative and culturally specific—not “chaos” to be aestheticised.

## Translation into original behaviours

- **After Dark** uses abstract paths, bridge pairs and signal points. The lines are invented and do not reproduce a Lagos map.
- **In Motion** uses an original market-word run, abstract ferry wake and generic route placard. No bus, market or ferry image is present.
- **Reassembled** uses generated paper planes, grid, halftone and scaffold rails. It does not imitate a named publication, fashion campaign or artwork.

## Cultural guardrail

Lagos is treated as a working city with infrastructure, trade, water, culture and authorship. None of the concepts presents it as a tourist spectacle, a synonym for disorder or a decorative “African” layer.
