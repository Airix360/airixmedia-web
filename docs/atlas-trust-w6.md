# Atlas Trust W6

## Scope boundary

W6 adds a private, no-index Trust review at `/internal/atlas-trust`. It uses the approved Edo identity and candidate art but changes no public route. Metadata specifies `noindex`, `nofollow`, `noarchive`, and `nosnippet`; the existing robots policy blocks `/internal/`; no public navigation links to the review.

## Experience

Trust is presented as accountability made visible: a contemporary record hall where evidence, responsibilities, maintenance, limits, and commitments are inspected. It is deliberately separate from the Studio compound and avoids testimonial walls, logo collections, score counters, shields, locks, handshakes, badges, certifications, and fake live-status interfaces.

The journey contains a six-entry Trust register, five evidence states, four responsibility boundaries, a maintenance-and-continuity statement, five known limitations, related routes, and the existing Discuss a Project action. Confidence is kept proportional to the available record.

## Verified basis and proposed disclosure

The page draws on repository publication-state logic, `PRODUCT.md`, `ACCESSIBILITY.md`, `SECURITY.md`, `src/lib/content.ts`, `src/lib/pages.ts`, public repository evidence, and Atlas governance. These sources establish product targets, stated practices, capability scope, and evidence models; they do not establish certification, compliance, universal controls, uptime, response times, backup terms, outcomes, or guarantees.

“Accountability, made visible.”, the record-hall metaphor, the register arrangement, evidence-state explanations, and connective language are proposed internal copy. Final entity details, legal language, jurisdictions, addresses, service commitments, security wording, and case-study outcomes still require verification.

## Components and state orientation

`TrustReview` composes `TrustArrival`, `TrustRegister`, `EvidenceStandard`, `ResponsibilityBoundary`, `KnownLimitations`, and `TrustReviewStatus`. The shared `AtlasStateMarker` preserves “Trust” as the public-facing identity while adding “Edo State / Stewardship Register” as contextual orientation and a return to `/atlas`. The state transition is secondary, quiet, and non-touristic.

## Candidate artwork

`ILL-0070` is a separate Edo Trust candidate depicting a fictional contemporary record hall, archive, courtyard, and visible review work. It is not compliance proof, a certification, a real facility, a client record, or an operational claim. Desktop and mobile compositions were generated separately. Only responsive WebP derivatives are loaded at runtime; source masters remain review material.

## Motion and reduced motion

Motion is limited to deliberate opening, placement, register reveal, and handoff cues. Trust meaning is never carried by animation. Reduced motion removes those finite transitions while retaining all six register entries, five evidence states, four responsibility boundaries, five limitations, and every action.

## Responsive and accessibility decisions

Desktop gives the record hall room to breathe while placing the accountability statement on the art’s right-side safe zone. Mobile uses a separately authored vertical scene, moves the copy plane below the calm upper field, and keeps identity, limitation status, meaning, and action in the opening viewport.

The route uses semantic landmarks, one `h1`, ordered headings, meaningful scene alternative text, hidden decorative material, visible focus, touch-safe navigation, written evidence states, a focusable skip target, no colour-only meaning, no hover-only disclosure, and reduced-motion equivalence.

## Known limitations

- Cultural, architectural, anatomy, document, cabinet, and crop review remains outstanding.
- The scene is illustrative and is not a verified place, archive, client engagement, or evidence system.
- Accessibility is a product target, not a certification.
- Maintenance, support, backup, recovery, privacy, and security commitments remain agreement- and implementation-specific.
- Case-study approval and outcome evidence remain incomplete; generated art is not proof.
- Passing checks is not visual, factual, legal, security, cultural, or owner approval.
